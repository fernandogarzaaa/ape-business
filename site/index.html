APE MCP

Any business can adopt its own APE, for a new business or an existing one. Connect it to your business, and the agent learns from that connection: your products, customers, orders, messages, and records, all inside your own tenant, never shared with another business. Fill in this onboarding flow and it makes your own APE.

How adopting works

1. Tell us about the business. New or existing, industry, and the job the agent should do.

2. Connect your business systems. Pick from the catalog. The agent starts read-only, discovers what each system holds, and builds a business profile for you to verify.

3. Gate every tool call with Sigil. Before a tool runs, its badge is checked. Unknown, bad signature, revoked, or above-policy tools never run.

4. Give the agent an identity. Name it, give it a phone number and an email address of its own, so customers can text it or email it like they would a staff member. That number and inbox belong to the agent, not to the owner's personal phone.

5. Set budget and safety caps. Steps, cost, and destructive actions, all capped.

6. Deploy your own APE. Run the bundle yourself, or request hosted provisioning when that service is live.

7. The agent learns, you verify. Corrections you make become the agent's memory. Nothing it learned becomes action until you confirm the profile.

Onboarding: make your own APE

This page saves nothing and sends nothing anywhere. Everything stays in your browser. Connectors below are a catalog to pick from. Only the web connector ships in APE today. The rest are connector templates your APE would add as YAML files, one per system, with an egress allowlist and an env referenced credential. Never paste a real secret into this page.

Step 1. Your business

Pick an industry above and the connector grid in Step 2 updates. You can still tick or untick anything.

Step 2. Connect your business systems

Tap a card to connect it. Start read-only. Writes always need confirmation. This is my suggestion order: connect the system of record first, then messages, then everything else.

Step 3. Budget, safety, and learning

Learning rule: discovery pass first, owner verifies the profile, then jobs use it. Owner corrections become memory. A job that cannot fully check itself ends as unverified. Treat that as a draft.

Step 4. How will you run your APE?

Self-run keeps your data and ledger on your own machine. Hosted provisioning would spin up one isolated APE per business, with its own endpoint, token, and ledger. That backend does not exist yet, so this page does not pretend to provision a live server.

Step 5. Give your agent an identity

This is the friction killer. Customers do not need a new app, an account, or to learn MCP. They text or email the agent, the same way they would text or email a person on your team.

Leave blank and the bundle suggests one from the agent name and business name. Use an address you control for the real agent, either on your own domain or a dedicated agent domain. Never use the owner's personal inbox as the agent inbox.

This is a number provisioned for the agent, not your personal number. In the real service it would be rented from an SMS gateway, one number per tenant, so you can keep it, change it, or disconnect it without touching your own phone. In this prototype, type the number you want shown.

The agent must identify itself as an AI agent in its first message, every time. It never pretends to be a human employee. Every text or email becomes one APE job, with a budget, a Sigil gate on every tool call, and a receipt in the ledger.

My connector suggestions, in order

1. System of record first. Shopify for a store, Salesforce for a sales business, your database for an operations business. The agent cannot learn your business without this.

2. Messages second. Gmail and Calendar. This is where customer intent, follow ups, and commitments actually live.

3. Team knowledge third. Slack, Sheets, GitHub. This is how the agent learns how your team already works.

4. Read-only first, always. Discovery pass, owner verified profile, then jobs. Writes and money moves need explicit confirmation, every time, until the owner raises the cap.

5. One credential per system, per tenant. OAuth where the service offers it (Gmail, Shopify, Salesforce). Env referenced tokens elsewhere. Never a shared key across businesses, never a secret in a profile file.

Security, in layers

1. One tenant, one agent, one vault. Your agent, ledger, secrets, and storage are yours alone. No shared memory, no shared keys, no learning from another business.

2. Sigil on every tool call. Before a tool runs, its badge is checked: signature, version, and revocation. Unknown, bad signature, revoked, or above-policy tools never run.

3. Egress allowlist. Each connector may only reach the hosts you allowed. Everything else is refused.

4. Sealbox content gate, Day 1. Before your data leaves your tenant to a third party, a local pass replaces names, emails, phone numbers, and client identifiers with tenant-scoped tokens, and only schema-fixed events cross. The token map lives only in your vault, never in a log. This is an integration into APE, not a separate product, and it ships in Phase 1, not as a later add-on.

5. Secrets stay secret. Profile and connector files carry env names only, never secret values. One credential per system, per tenant, revocable on disconnect.

6. Audit you can export. Every job has a run ID, an outcome, and a receipt. You can export your ledger, and a stop button stops a task.

7. Humans gate the stakes. Spend above a cap, money moves, commitments to third parties, and destructive actions always need a human, no matter how trusted the agent is.

Full design, including what Sealbox does not mitigate and what we may not claim yet, lives in the SECURITY.md design document for this product. It is not published yet, and neither is this page.

Honest limits

  • GitHub Pages hosts static pages only. It cannot run the APE agent runtime, connect to your Shopify or Salesforce account, or provision a live server.
  • Only the web connector ships in APE today. Shopify, Salesforce, Gmail, database, and the rest are catalog templates here, not shipped connectors yet.
  • The Connect and learn button is a simulation. It shows the shape of discovery, not your real products, orders, or emails.
  • Learning stays inside one tenant. Your agent must never learn from another business's data, and disconnecting a system must revoke its access and stop future reads.
  • APE's A2A bridge is v1: synchronous request and response plus polling, no streaming and no push notifications.
  • Sealbox is a Day 1 integration in Phase 1, not a live gate in this prototype. The design exists, the redaction pass is not yet wired into an APE job, so do not read this page as claiming PII never leaves a business today.
  • Phone numbers and inboxes in this wizard are placeholders you type, not provisioned numbers or mailboxes. A real agent number needs an SMS gateway rental per tenant, and a real agent inbox needs inbound email handling. Both add per-tenant cost and, in the US, SMS registration rules. Budget for them in pricing before selling text or email access.
  • The agent must identify itself as an AI agent in its first message. It never presents as a human employee.
